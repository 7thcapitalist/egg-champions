## Generations and limits

- **Generations used:** 21 models produced, 2 or 3 attempts for every pet except Steamtoad and Ashwolf, which passed on attempt 1.
- **Jobs submitted:** 30. The 9 that failed produced nothing.
- **Character limit:** 7 jobs failed with "Character limit exceeded". The prompt cap appears to be about 500 characters; every prompt I trimmed to under roughly 490 went through.
- **Rate limit:** 2 jobs failed with "Failed to publish assets: Too Many Requests", both while I had 3 running at once. After I dropped to 2 at a time there were no more.
- **Classifier timeouts:** 2 tool calls (one screenshot, one generation) were refused by a safety-classifier timeout. The generation went through on retry.
- **Studio:** stayed connected throughout. Nothing was deleted, saved or published, and I touched only my own models.

## Suggested rule changes

1. State the prompt cap: keep prompts under about 480 characters. The full style block plus the quadruped line plus a negative list does not fit, so give a short required core instead, for example "battle monster ... Stylized game monster, clean shapes, bold colors. Not cute."
2. Cap concurrency at 2 per agent when three agents share the place.
3. For birds and bats, do not write "standing upright" or use "monster" without an anatomy anchor. Add "real eagle shape, feathered bird body, no arms, not a dragon, not humanoid". This fixed Magmaroc. It did not fix the bat, which came back either as a gargoyle with arms or as a chubby toy.
4. Scorpions are unreliable. "Pincers raised high" made the model replace the tail with a second pair of pincers, twice. Describe the pincers neutrally and plan a texture or manual fix for the yellow joints and bolt stinger.
5. "Legendary" does not add elemental density. "Covered in dozens of spines" gave five cacti. Counts and placement ("rows of spines along every shell plate") worked slightly better.
6. A roaring open mouth or snarl is the most reliable anti-cute lever. "Angry eyes" alone still gave a smiling tortoise.
7. Black comes out dark grey and "glowing" colors come out pale. Ask for "vivid bright orange and yellow" and "bold saturated colors", and expect a texture pass for true black.
8. For stance, describe body geometry ("belly almost touching the ground, front legs stretched forward, head low", "body level and horizontal like a weasel, front paws on the ground"). "Alert stance, head raised" made the otter stand up.
9. Models facing -Z after zero rotation was consistent across all 21, so that can be written into the process.
10. Target height includes tall effects (geyser, cactus, steam, spread wings), which shrinks the body. Consider a body-height target for pets with tall effects.
